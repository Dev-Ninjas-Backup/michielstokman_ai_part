I said quietly, more to the light than to them: “Maybe another child will come. Maybe not. But I’m done waiting for permission to be whole.”

Sofia squeezed my hand once. Lena lifted her head, met my eyes for a long moment, then settled again. No more words.

That evening we sat by the small fire we lit as the air cooled again. Flames moved gently. I felt their shoulders against mine. No plans. No answers. Only the three of us—skin still sun-warmed, hair drying in soft waves—holding space together without demand.

I closed my eyes. Silence filled the room, warm, wide, generous. For the first time in years it did not feel like a hole. It felt like home.

And that was enough.
